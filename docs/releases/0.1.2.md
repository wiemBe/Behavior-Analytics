# Behavior Analytics 0.1.2

A feature release on top of 0.1.1: property watches, a console-wide read of QRadar's stored
past, and a faster default search cadence. Five migrations come with it, so the schema head
moves from `0025` to `0030`. Nothing starts on its own. Property-watch collection is off until
an operator turns it on, and no history is read until the question is answered.

## What is new for an analyst

- **Property watches.** A watch follows the values of one or two QRadar properties over a log
  source, a log source type or a log source group, and reports the values that changed: a value
  above its seasonal band (spike), a value below its band while it normally carries traffic
  (drop), a value that stopped (disappearance), a value that was not seen before (new value), and
  a combination of two known values that was not seen before (a watch with two properties). The
  values an operator pins are always counted, even when they carry no traffic; a pinned value is
  never reported as new. The pages are `/behavior/watches` (the list), `/behavior/watches/new`
  (the editor) and `/behavior/watches/[id]` (one watch: its values, its coverage, its incidents
  and the read-only AQL behind it). A watch is also a Dashboard widget, "Property watch", with its
  pinned values and the top values of the last bucket. Property-watch incidents appear in
  `/anomalies` under their own taxonomy.
- **Reading the past from QRadar's history.** The platform may search the console's stored past
  once, to fill the baselines it would otherwise fill one week at a time: source volume, the
  catch-all senders list, WAF, IPS and rule matches. A property watch may ask for its own past
  separately, when it is created or before it has collected anything. A bucket read from the
  past is marked where it is shown: the charts and the tables say "read from QRadar history, not
  judged".
- **Nothing read from history is ever judged.** A history bucket trains a baseline and nothing
  else. It opens no incident, no breach and no alert, and the interval it covers is never
  reported as a deviation. A watch that read its own past does not start its learning period
  again: the history it read is that period, and a detector that needs more history than was
  read stays `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` until the live buckets add the missing visits.
- **A faster default cadence.** Every search family now searches every five minutes by default,
  except WAF and IPS, which default to fifteen minutes. Before this release the default of every
  family was one hour, and a cadence an operator has set stays as it was set: only the untouched
  defaults moved. A site that never set one sends many more searches to QRadar now.

## What is new for an operator

- **The install asks once whether the past may be read.** The last question of a first install
  is "Read the last 28 days of history from QRadar now, so baselines are steadier from day 1?".
  A scripted install answers it with `HISTORY_BACKFILL=yes` or `HISTORY_BACKFILL=no` in the
  answers file. An absent key is neither a "no" nor a guess: the console stays undecided, and
  the first administrator sign-in asks the same question in the interface. An upgrade never
  asks it.
- **The decision and the progress are on `/admin/history-backfill`.** The page shows the answer,
  who gave it and when, and one row per family and scope with the window asked, the window
  proved, the floor, the counters and the reason. It can ask every family to read now and to
  stop. A "No" may become a "Yes" later; a "Yes" is never reversed, the reading is stopped
  instead. A WAF or IPS source that has already collected live data cannot read its past: only a
  source enabled after the answer is read, and the page says so per source.
- **The cost of a reading.** While a reading runs, the platform sends read-only searches to
  QRadar. The live collection of the same family runs first, and the reading spends what is
  left of the tick budget. A reading may take at most 72 hours
  (`HISTORY_BACKFILL_MAX_HOURS`); a family whose window does not fit that budget reads a shorter
  window and says so ("window reduced from 28 to 20 days: the estimate exceeded 72 hours"). The
  estimate is shown before the answer and on the page. It is a model, not a promise of a finish
  time.
- **The new settings** are in the configuration reference (`docs/CONFIGURATION.md`): the
  `HISTORY_BACKFILL_*` settings for the console-wide reading, and the `PW_BOOTSTRAP_*` settings
  for a property watch's own reading. Both switches are on by default, and both act only after
  a decision is recorded.
- **Before relying on history on a production console, confirm its retention buckets with the
  console's owner.** The platform proves how far back it can read from the console itself, but
  it assumes that deletion inside a scope is oldest-first and that a retention class holds
  what it should. A console whose retention buckets are configured differently may hold less
  than the window asks for; the reading then ends `PARTIAL` with the reason, and nothing before
  the proved floor is written.

**Schema.** Five additive migrations: `0026` (property watches), `0027` (a custom rule may pin
a property-watch value), `0028` (the untouched search-cadence defaults move to five and fifteen
minutes), `0029` (the property-watch history columns) and `0030` (the history backfill: the
decision, the scope state and the `origin` marker). The head is `0030`. They add tables, columns
and one data change: `0026` and `0029` build the property-watch tables, `0027` adds one column to
the custom rules and widens their subject check, `0028` moves the untouched cadence rows, and
`0030` adds the history backfill's two tables and an `origin` marker to four bucket tables. No
stored Phase A column is touched. A downgrade of `0030` deletes the rows that were read from the
past, because they cannot be told apart once the marker is gone, and the two history tables with
them; every live row, every baseline and every incident stays. The rollback path is
`./qradar-dash rollback` with the pre-upgrade backup, not an Alembic downgrade.

**Upgrade.**

```bash
git fetch --tags && git checkout v0.1.2
./qradar-dash upgrade 0.1.2
```

The upgrade is allowed from `0.1.1`. A site on `0.1.0` must be brought to `0.1.1` first: the
upgrade stops with `upgrade refused: 0.1.2 requires at least 0.1.1` and changes nothing, so
the step through `0.1.1` is an ordinary upgrade of its own. The notes of `0.1.1`
(`docs/releases/0.1.1.md`) describe that step, including the site whose first install failed
its QRadar check. The upgrade takes a backup before it migrates and stops the writers first.
If it fails, the old release is started again and the backup is there to restore from.

The upgraded site is not asked about history. The question belongs to the first administrator
sign-in, or to the installer of a new site, and until it is answered nothing is read from the
past. The new families stay off: `PW_COLLECTION_ENABLED` and `PW_ALERTING_ENABLED` are off
until an operator enables them, and the WAF, IPS and rule-match collection switches are
unchanged. The image prefix moves by itself: the `0.1.0` and `0.1.1` images stay under the
old name, and the `0.1.2` images are pulled under the new one.
