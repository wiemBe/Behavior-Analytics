## 2. What was decided (summary)

- **Marker.** `pw_bucket.origin` (`LIVE`/`HISTORY`). Three independent guards keep a history bucket from ever being pending: (1) `collected_through` and `evaluated_through` stay NULL while the watch is `READING` (`pending_buckets` needs `bucket_start <= collected_through`, repositories:200; training needs `evaluated_through IS NOT NULL`, :540); (2) both are seeded to the last history bucket at hand-off; (3) `pending_buckets` adds `origin = 'LIVE'`. F.1's PW loader also filters on it (it reads buckets with no regard to the watch's watermarks, loaders:486-560, and its watermark is per subject kind). Every reader of `pw_bucket` is classified LIVE-only, EVIDENCE or DISPLAY, and a guard test fails on an unclassified one. History buckets are also never used as the judged interval: the disappearance streak reads live buckets only, and a value first seen in history is "established".
- **Hand-off.** Oldest-first, one step per tick, ending at the closed-bucket boundary of the tick that finishes it (a moving end, so no catch-up replay). At hand-off `collected_through = evaluated_through = last history bucket`; the existing live path then starts at the next bucket (`through + step`, pw_collector:356). Zero gap, zero overlap by construction; a half-done tick just resumes from `history_through`; overlapping ticks are stopped by a per-watch advisory lock; everything of a step is one transaction. The history path runs the same fold/admission/persistence functions as live (equivalence test), with `retire_stale` per bucket.
- **Ariel retention is proved, not assumed.** The first step runs the existing TOTALS search (5-min buckets, spans of up to 168 h) from `T0 - 24 h`; the first bucket with events `b0` gives a floor `F = max(T0, b0 + margin)`. Nothing before `F` is written (no zero, no `NOT_COLLECTED` row); a zero after `F` is measured silence; no event at all gives `UNAVAILABLE`. It costs no extra search when the scope has events at the window start. It rests on assumption A1 (oldest-first deletion within one retention class) which only the console can confirm (probes P-1 to P-8, 40 searches, 6 GETs).
- **Cost table** (H.4.5, 12 history searches per tick, 5-min cadence, measured 1 to 3.5 s per search): 6 values: 3 / 7 / 22 searches for 1 / 7 / 28 days (1, 1, 2 ticks); 200 values (worst case): 17 / 104 / 413 searches (2, 9, 35 ticks = 10 min, 45 min, 2 h 55 min; 35 h at the old 1 h cadence). Ariel time above 8 h of grouped data is unmeasured.
- **Settings** (H.10; eight, all new): `pw_bootstrap_enabled` **false**, `pw_bootstrap_days` 28 (between `pw_new_value_history_days` and `pw_baseline_lookback_days`), `pw_bootstrap_searches_per_tick` 12, `pw_bootstrap_seconds_per_tick` 180, `pw_bootstrap_floor_margin_hours` 24, `pw_bootstrap_max_hours` 72, `pw_bootstrap_max_failures` 3, `pw_bootstrap_max_lower_bound_share` 0.05. Per-watch `history_days`. Off by default because three production-console facts are unknown (retention buckets, payload retention, wide-search time) and it spends unrequested Ariel searches.
- **State.** Migration `0029` (renumbered by the orchestrator; T-134 holds `0028`): history columns on `property_watch`, `pw_bucket.origin`, a `READING_HISTORY` watch status (recreated `CHECK`). No staging table (it would persist un-admitted value texts, rule 3). Existing enabled watches are not bootstrapped at upgrade; history can be requested only for a watch with no stored bucket of its current version.
- **Fail closed** (H.6, 13 rows): retention gap, coverage below the floor in old events (excluded from training and fails the new-value history check for history buckets), truncated or failed chunk (failed halves the window, a gap after 3 tries), today's membership applied to the past (stated), admission caps (bucket flags as live), a source that did not exist yet, scope states, time limit, budget starvation, switch off, density gate.
- **Training** (H.8): untouched; 6 statements per chunk whatever the window; the rows grow (up to 806,000 metric rows per chunk, the day-28 steady state reached at day 1), so the build selects columns instead of ORM entities. Cells are reliable at hand-off after a 7-day window (12 samples per hour-of-week visit, `baseline_min_samples` 8), with weaker statistics than the 28-day default (four visits).

## 3. Where the code and docs/PHASE-G-PROPERTY-WATCH.md disagree (the code wins)

1. `verify_value_history` (history.py:12-82) takes no coverage input; §G.5 says any verdict that reads an absence needs coverage at the floor. A live parsing break followed by recovery lets a returning value read as new. The design adds the check for history buckets only (Q5).
2. `watch_first_collected_at` is the minimum stored bucket including a `NOT_COLLECTED` row (repositories:178-186); §G.8.3 says "first collected bucket".
3. §G.10.1 "Enabling again starts fresh": `PATCH` resets no watermark (routes:731-863), so a watch disabled for days re-enables and backfills 13 buckets per tick from the old watermark into evaluation. A replay of the kind D-27 forbids for history; reported, not changed.
4. A `watch_version` bump keeps `collected_through`/`evaluated_through` (routes:826-828); the design resets them for a watch with `history_days`.
5. Windows have a 168 h hard bound (bucketed_metrics.py:28) that Phase G never mentions.
6. `_run_bounded_aggregate` gives a search 120 s with a fixed 1 s poll (qradar_rest.py:2114); `ariel_poll_interval_seconds` (config.py:127) is not used there; §G.14.1 gives only the 0.4 to 3.5 s measurements.
7. `admission_truncated` is set by `rejected > 0` **or** the fold's cut (admission.py:204-213), including values below the threshold; §G.7.3 describes it only as the ceiling binding. A bucket with more than `pw_max_values_per_bucket` distinct untracked values is never valid history.
8. `pw_bucket`'s primary key is `(watch_id, bucket_start)` without `watch_version` (models:209-212); §G.7.1 says buckets are stamped with the version. A rewritten window overwrites the other version's rows (harmless to readers).
9. `dispatch_property_watch_collection` has no advisory lock (tasks.py:1394-1442) while other families have one; a history step is long enough to need it.
10. Cadence default 1 h in the migration seed (0026:258) and §G.3 versus D-28's 5 min (T-134 pending).
11. The static enable cost counts steady state only (services/property_watches.py:407-415); the bootstrap's draw is outside it by design.
12. SEARCH-CADENCE §SC.5 item 2 bounds a tick's window to `cadence/bucket + backfill` buckets; a history step is a deliberate, budgeted exception.
13. PW-3b's recorded bound of 806,000 training rows "not measured on a console"; the bootstrap reaches it on day 1.

## 8. Open points (what could go wrong if left)

- **A1 is an assumption.** If a scope's events fall in retention buckets of different lengths, a history window can look complete while missing a class. Mitigation in the design: the switch is off by default and the page states the assumption; probes P-3/P-1/P-2 are what close it. Not detectable at run time.
- **Ariel time on wide windows is unmeasured.** The cost table uses the measured 1 to 3.5 s; a 168 h custom-property search may take far longer or hit the 120 s cut. The planner shrinks windows on failure, so the effect is slower reading, not wrong data.
- **Delete during a long step.** The collector does not lock the watch row; a delete during a history step fails the instance's write at the foreign key and rolls back the whole tick (the same exposure as a live tick, longer). Not designed away.
- **ADD COLUMN `origin NOT NULL` on a hypertable with compressed chunks** depends on the TimescaleDB version; PW-13a must test it (the design says so).
- **Training memory.** 100 values x 8,064 buckets of ORM metric rows in one statement; the design changes the trainer to select columns but the real memory is not measured.
- **The demonstration needs 8 days of events** for source 212 (or another scope); unknown until P-1. The three cases of H.12.1 cover it; case C shows `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` rather than a detection.
- **History starves** when live watches use the whole tick budget; visible ("waiting for search budget"), never a wrong verdict.
- The PW-10 numbers used are from an in-progress report; I did not wait for its final version.
